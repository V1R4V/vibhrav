Understand your strain
What strain means for your health

Strain is how much work your heart did over the day. Regular moderate and vigorous activity is one of the strongest levers on long-term health: it raises cardio fitness, which is closely tied to living longer.

Guidelines suggest at least 150 minutes of moderate or 75 minutes of vigorous activity a week. More helps, up to the point where you stop recovering from it.

How strain is scored

Every heart-rate reading is placed between your resting rate and your maximum (178 bpm). Minutes at higher intensity count for much more than easy minutes, and the total is squeezed onto a 0 to 21 scale.

The scale flattens at the top: going from 4 to 8 takes a brisk walk, going from 16 to 18 takes a long, hard session. Workouts added 53 load on an average day in this period and the rest of the day added 0.

For lifting, heart rate under-reports the effort, so effort rating × duration is used when it is higher.

What raises it
Time in zones 4 and 5 (above 80% of your range): intervals, matches, hard climbs.
Long sessions, even at moderate intensity.
An active day around the workout: walking, stairs, standing work.
What keeps it low, and when high is bad
Sedentary days and short, easy sessions keep strain under 8.
Not wearing the watch: missing hours cannot be counted and the day is marked partial.
High strain is not bad in itself. It becomes a problem when it stays well above your usual for a week or more while recovery sits in the red.
How to use it

Match strain to recovery: about 14 to 18 on a green day, 10 to 14 on yellow, under 10 on red. Across a week, mix two or three hard days with easy ones rather than making every day medium. In this period you averaged 217 moderate or harder minutes a week, above the 150 that guidelines suggest.
