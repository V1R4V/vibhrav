Understand your health score
What this number means

There are two versions. The daily score rates one day: that night's sleep and that day's movement. A rest day with poor sleep scores low; a good night plus a long workout scores high.

The period score covers the range you choose at the top: each area is averaged over every day in it that has data, then the areas are combined. It is compared with the period of the same length just before. A day with no watch data is left out, not counted as zero; a day the watch was worn with no exercise counts as 0 exercise minutes. Days before age 18 are left out, because the studies behind every weight followed adults. It is a summary to track, not a diagnosis.

The line on the chart below is always the 28-day score, so you can see how the score moved inside the period.

How it is calculated

Four areas are each scored 0 to 100, then combined. An area counts for more when research shows it predicts a longer life more strongly.

Cardio fitness, 35%. The median of your VO2 max readings in the period, placed among people of your age and sex using the FRIEND registry's treadmill percentiles (Kaminsky 2015). The median person scores 60, the 25th percentile 46 and the 75th 74. Across 37 studies, the fittest third of adults had about half the death rate of the least fit third. The often-quoted "5 times" compares the bottom quarter with the top 2%; compared like for like, fitness, steps and exercise have effects of the same size. Fitness keeps the largest weight because a treadmill test is the hardest measurement of the three.
Activity, 30%. Daily steps against 10,000, weekly exercise minutes, and how often you work out. The quarter of people who walked most had about half the death rate of the quarter who walked least. Exercise minutes are scored by how much risk they remove: 150 a week scores 60, 375 scores 94 and 600 scores 100.
Sleep, 25%. Your sleep score, which leans on how long and how regularly you sleep. The most regular fifth of sleepers had a 30% lower death rate than the least regular fifth.
Resting heart, 10%. 50 bpm or lower scores 100, and each bpm above that takes away 2.5 points. Every 10 bpm higher goes with about 9% more risk.
Recovery is not part of this score. It was 10% until November 2026. It describes one morning against your own usual, sleep and heart rate are already counted above, and no study links wrist HRV to how long people live. It stays as its own daily score.

On a single day, activity uses that day's steps against 10,000, exercise minutes against 45, and workout time against 45. If an area has no data it is left out and the others are scaled up.

How sure this is. The order of the weights is well supported. The exact percentages are a judgement: the studies followed different groups of people, most of them over 50, and fitness, activity and resting heart rate partly measure the same thing.

Sources: Laukkanen 2022, Mayo Clinic Proceedings, 37 cohorts (fitness); Mandsager 2018, JAMA Network Open (fitness groups); Paluch 2022, Lancet Public Health (steps); Ekelund 2019, BMJ, and Arem 2015, JAMA Internal Medicine (exercise); Windred 2024, Sleep (regularity); Zhang 2016, CMAJ and Jensen 2013, Heart (resting heart rate).

What raises it
Better cardio fitness. It is the largest part of the score, and it also lowers Body Age and resting heart rate.
8,000 or more steps a day and at least 150 minutes of exercise a week.
Seven or more hours of sleep, at the same times each day.
An easy day after each hard one, so recovery stays above 67%.
What lowers it
Weeks with little exercise, which lower fitness over time.
Few steps and long stretches of sitting.
Short sleep, or sleep times that change from day to day.
Stress, illness, alcohol or late meals, which lower HRV and raise heart rate overnight.
