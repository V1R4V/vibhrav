Understand your sleep score
Why sleep matters for your health

Sleep is when your body repairs muscle, files away memory and resets heart rate and blood pressure. Too little of it, or sleep at shifting hours, shows up the next day as a higher resting heart rate, lower HRV, a worse mood and slower reactions.

Over years, short and irregular sleep is linked with higher risk of heart disease, weight gain and early death. In a 61,000-person study, how regular sleep was predicted that risk better than how long it was.

How your sleep score is built

Six parts, each scored 0 to 100, then weighted. Your averages over these 30 nights:

Sleep amount, 35% (61/100). Hours asleep, naps included, against your personal need of 8h 33m. The closer to your need, the higher it goes; it tops out once the need is met.
Regularity, 25% (100/100). How closely your asleep and awake hours match from one day to the next.
Continuity, 25% (87/100). Less time awake in the night and fewer long wake-ups score higher.
Timing, overnight heart rate and stages, 5% each (98/100, 62/100, 100/100).

More deep and REM sleep does raise the score, but only a little. The watch misses about half of true deep sleep, so leaning on it would make the score less trustworthy, not more.

What makes it good
Sleeping your full need (8h 33m for you). Hard training days and built-up debt raise the need.
Going to bed and getting up at about the same time, weekends included.
Sleeping through, with few wake-ups longer than five minutes.
A heart rate that drops early in the night and stays low.
What makes it bad
Short nights. Missed sleep is carried as debt (1.3 h on your latest night) and is repaid slowly.
Bedtimes that swing by more than an hour, and long weekend lie-ins.
Alcohol, a heavy meal or hard exercise close to bedtime: they fragment sleep and keep heart rate up.
A warm or bright room, and caffeine after early afternoon.
What to do next

Your weakest part in this period is sleep amount at 61/100. Aim to be in bed by a time that allows 8h 33m of sleep, and move bedtime earlier by 15 minutes a night until you get there.
