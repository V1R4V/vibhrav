Understand your cardio health
Why cardio fitness matters most

VO2 max is the most oxygen your body can use during hard exercise. Of everything a watch measures, it is among the strongest predictors of a long life. In 122,007 adults, the least fit quarter had about 5 times the death rate of the fittest 2 to 3 in 100, and no level was found where more fitness stopped helping (Mandsager 2018).

Across 33 studies, each 3.5 mL/kg/min higher was linked with about 13% lower risk of death from any cause (Kodama 2009). That is why cardio fitness is 35% of your health score and the largest part of Body Age.

How these numbers are made

VO2 max is Apple's estimate from heart rate and pace on outdoor walks, runs and hikes. It reads about 6 mL/kg/min low in fit adults (Lambe 2025), so Nura adds up to 4 back before comparing you with others. Your place among people of your age and sex uses the FRIEND registry's treadmill results (Kaminsky 2015).

Cardio recovery is how many beats your heart rate drops in the first minute after a workout. A drop of 12 bpm or less after a treadmill test was linked with higher risk of death in a study of 2,428 adults (Cole 1999). The watch measures it after the workout, not on a treadmill, so treat it as a guide.

Resting heart rate: every 10 bpm higher goes with about 9% more risk (Zhang 2016). Breathing rate and blood oxygen are read while you sleep. They are most useful against your own usual: a rise in breathing rate can come before you feel ill.

What raises cardio fitness
One or two sessions a week of hard intervals, such as 4 rounds of 4 minutes at a hard pace with 3 easy minutes between.
Two or three easy sessions of 30 minutes or more where you can still talk.
Outdoor walks and runs with your watch, so it can update VO2 max at all.
What lowers it
Weeks without exercise: fitness starts to drop within a few weeks of stopping.
Weight gain, since VO2 max is per kilogram.
Poor sleep and illness, which raise resting and sleeping heart rate.
Your best next step

You are at or above the target for your age. Keep two or three cardio sessions a week to hold it, and one hard one to keep raising it.

Sources: Mandsager 2018, JAMA Network Open; Kodama 2009, JAMA; Kaminsky 2015, Mayo Clinic Proceedings (FRIEND); Lambe 2025 (Apple VO2 max accuracy); Cole 1999, New England Journal of Medicine; Zhang 2016, CMAJ.
